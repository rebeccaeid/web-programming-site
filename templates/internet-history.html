Introduction

The history of the Internet is the story of transforming isolated, specialized computing systems into a unified global communications platform through foundational technical decisions, open standards, and architectural layers designed to scale independently of upper-layer applications.

Milestones in Internet Infrastructure

1969: First ARPANET Connection

The Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA) established the first host-to-host packet-switched connection between UCLA and Stanford Research Institute.

Source: Internet Society

1971: First Network Email

Ray Tomlinson created the first experimental network email system over ARPANET, introducing the '@' symbol to target specific hosts.

Source: Internet Hall of Fame

1974: TCP Specification (RFC 675)

Vint Cerf, Yogen Dalal, and Carl Sunshine published RFC 675, defining the initial specification for the Transmission Control Program.

Source: IETF RFC Editor

1983: Flag Day & TCP/IP Standard

ARPANET officially transitioned from NCP to the TCP/IP protocol suite on January 1, establishing modern network architecture.

Source: Internet Hall of Fame

1983–1984: Domain Name System (DNS)

Paul Mockapetris introduced DNS to replace the single centralized HOSTS.TXT file with a distributed hierarchical naming scheme.

Source: IETF RFC Editor

1989: Border Gateway Protocol (BGP)

BGP was introduced to handle routing between disparate autonomous systems and competing commercial networks.

Source: IETF RFC Editor

1995: NSFNET Decommissioning & Commercialization

The NSFNET backbone was officially retired, completing the structural transition of internet routing to private commercial backbones.

Source: Internet Society
